# narod-2002

Rules for generating Russian personal and fan-made websites in the spirit of Narod.ru and the broader Runet of roughly 2000–2004.

## Purpose

Generate a believable **handmade personal, hobby, fan, student, or small community website** from the early-2000s Russian web.

The result must feel like a site made by a person around their identity, hobby, fandom, studies, friends, or small community — not like a modern product page with a retro skin.

The defining trait is **authorial DIY structure**, not one fixed Narod.ru template.

## Period

Primary target: **2000–2004**.

Default mental model: a site designed for a desktop browser, often with 800×600 in mind, built under slow-connection constraints, edited manually, and updated in small increments over time.

## Suitable site types

Use this archetype for:

- personal homepages;
- hobby sites;
- fan sites;
- student/group pages;
- amateur reference sites;
- small communities built around one author or narrow topic;
- personal sites that grew into small hobby portals.

Do not use it for:

- large portals such as Yandex/Rambler/Mail.ru;
- professional news media;
- phpBB/Invision-style forums;
- corporate promo sites;
- uCoz-style modular sites from the later 2000s.

## Core principle

**Design follows the author or fandom.**

Do not begin from a generic “retro web” visual kit. First infer what the author cares about, then let that topic drive section names, palette, images, decorative motifs, and microcopy.

A Star Wars fan page, a student-group site, a poetry homepage, and a programmer’s personal site may all belong to this archetype while looking very different.

## Visual intensity

Support one archetype with three intensity levels instead of creating separate sub-archetypes.

### `plain`

Mostly text and links.

Use:

- white or simple solid background;
- system fonts;
- text navigation;
- one-column or simple two-column layout;
- few images;
- minimal borders and decoration.

Appropriate for technical, academic, reference, or utilitarian personal sites.

### `decorated`

The default.

Add some of:

- simple background color or repeating background image;
- small photos or GIF/JPG illustrations;
- custom separators;
- simple bordered panels;
- a counter, rating badge, or one small banner area;
- small graphic navigation elements.

Keep the page visibly handmade rather than professionally art-directed.

### `expressive`

For fan, music, gaming, celebrity, fantasy, or strongly personal sites.

May include:

- dark or saturated thematic background;
- bright accent colors;
- custom raster headings;
- GIF decoration;
- 88×31 buttons;
- awards and affiliates;
- themed sidebars;
- browser/resolution badges;
- stronger asymmetry and more visible handcraft.

Even here, decoration must be **topic-driven**, not random noise.

## Layout

Prefer simple desktop layouts.

Typical structures:

1. single main column;
2. narrow left or right navigation + main content;
3. header + content + narrow sidebar;
4. manually grouped blocks rather than a strict modern grid.

Guidelines:

- fixed-width or visually constrained layout is preferred;
- around 740–800 px is a strong default for early-period pages;
- allow uneven spacing and imperfect alignment;
- small tables may be used in `AUTHENTIC` mode for layout;
- do not make the composition perfectly responsive or geometrically polished;
- a personal site may expand into many sections without becoming a modern portal.

## Typography

Prefer browser-safe system fonts:

- Arial;
- Verdana;
- Tahoma;
- Times New Roman.

Rules:

- body text should feel small by modern standards;
- use normal browser-like text hierarchy rather than oversized display typography;
- links may remain visibly underlined;
- blue links and purple visited links are historically appropriate, especially in `plain` and `decorated` modes;
- text-heavy pages and long link lists are acceptable;
- avoid modern font pairing systems and oversized headings.

Do not hard-code one exact font size as historically universal.

## Colors and backgrounds

There is no universal Narod palette.

Choose colors from the site’s subject:

- technical/academic → light background, restrained palette;
- music/celebrity → saturated thematic colors, photo-driven accents;
- sci-fi/gaming → dark background and bright accent colors are acceptable;
- personal/family → soft or idiosyncratic author-selected colors.

Allowed:

- solid background colors;
- simple repeating raster backgrounds;
- textured or tiled backgrounds;
- high-contrast combinations that would feel too bold today.

Avoid applying neon colors merely because the page is “retro”.

## Images and decoration

Use small raster assets rather than large modern imagery.

Preferred:

- small GIFs;
- small JPG photos;
- decorative headers;
- separators;
- tiny icons;
- small themed illustrations;
- badges and counters;
- occasional animated GIFs.

Images should reflect early-web bandwidth constraints.

Do not use:

- huge hero images;
- full-width photography;
- retina-sharp modern stock imagery;
- large SVG illustration systems;
- modern icon libraries as the main visual language.

## 88×31 buttons and link exchange

88×31 buttons are a real social mechanism of the period, not merely decoration.

For `decorated` or `expressive` pages, optionally include:

- 1–3 buttons for friends, partner sites, or the author’s own site;
- a “Друзья сайта”, “Ссылки”, “Наши друзья”, or similar section;
- optional copyable HTML code for linking back to the site.

Do not fill every page with a wall of buttons.

## Navigation

Navigation should feel authored, not standardized.

Common items:

- Главная;
- Обо мне;
- Новости / Что нового;
- Фотки / Фотоальбом;
- Друзья;
- Ссылки;
- Гостевая;
- Творчество;
- Стихи / Проза;
- Мои работы;
- Форум;
- Карта сайта;
- тематические sections named in the author’s own language.

Prefer idiosyncratic labels when they fit the topic.

Do not normalize everything to modern labels like “About”, “Portfolio”, “Resources”, “Contact”.

## Content patterns

A believable page should usually contain some signs of ongoing manual maintenance.

Useful patterns:

- dated updates;
- “Что нового” section;
- “Последнее обновление” date;
- notes about newly added photos or sections;
- unfinished or planned sections;
- version labels such as “Version 0.2”;
- short author comments about redesigns;
- requests to sign the guestbook;
- personal remarks, slang, emoticons, and informal phrasing.

The site may openly look unfinished.

## Social layer

Choose 1–4 of these depending on the site:

- guestbook;
- e-mail;
- ICQ number;
- small forum;
- poll;
- awards;
- affiliates;
- friends/partner sites;
- visitor counter;
- rating badges;
- “picture of the week”;
- birthdays or community notices.

These are functional parts of the site, not decorative stickers.

### Guestbook

Treat the guestbook as a real page with visible messages.

Typical fields:

- name;
- e-mail;
- website URL;
- message;
- date/time.

The archive of messages should be visible and may include link exchange, feedback, corrections, or casual conversation.

## Counters and ratings

Optional historical markers include:

- HotLog;
- Rambler Top100;
- Рейтинг@Mail.ru;
- simple visitor counters.

Use sparingly. One page does not need every service at once.

## Browser and resolution notes

Optional for `decorated` and especially `expressive` variants:

- “Оптимизировано для 800×600”;
- “Лучше смотреть в Internet Explorer”;
- IE/Netscape-style badge;
- small compatibility note in the footer.

These should feel like period-authentic service notes, not jokes about old browsers.

## Microcopy style

Prefer personal, direct Russian phrasing.

Examples of tone:

- “Добавила новые фотки”;
- “Пишите в гостевую”;
- “Наконец-то обновил раздел”;
- “Сайт пока в разработке”;
- “Заходите ещё”;
- “Поменяла дизайн — как вам?”;
- “Последнее обновление: 14.09.2003”.

Do not imitate these exact phrases mechanically. Preserve the informal, hand-written feeling.

## Deliberate imperfection

Allow:

- uneven block spacing;
- dense text areas;
- mixed text and graphic navigation;
- inconsistent but understandable section naming;
- small visual mismatches;
- partial redesign remnants;
- “under construction” areas.

Do not intentionally break usability. The page should remain navigable.

## Forbidden modern patterns

Avoid:

- hero sections;
- large rounded cards;
- glassmorphism;
- large border radii;
- modern SaaS gradients;
- mobile-first app shells;
- sticky mobile navigation;
- hamburger menus;
- oversized whitespace;
- modern dashboard cards;
- Lucide/Heroicons-style icon systems;
- large CTA buttons such as “Начать бесплатно”;
- perfectly consistent design-token spacing;
- generic AI-generated marketing copy.

## Generation procedure

When generating a page with this archetype:

1. Determine the site owner and their main interest.
2. Choose `plain`, `decorated`, or `expressive`.
3. Select one dominant visual motif based on the author/topic.
4. Choose 4–7 primary sections.
5. Add 1–3 period social/service elements where appropriate.
6. Add dated/manual-maintenance cues.
7. Keep imagery small and bandwidth-conscious.
8. Keep the layout desktop-oriented and visibly handmade.
9. Remove any modern UI pattern that slipped in.

## Authentic vs modern implementation

### `AUTHENTIC`

May use:

- table-based layout;
- hard-coded widths;
- presentational HTML where historically appropriate;
- older markup patterns;
- decorative raster assets.

### `MODERN_ENGINE`

Use modern HTML/CSS internally while preserving the same visual outcome.

Requirements:

- do not modernize the visible composition;
- retain fixed-width desktop character where relevant;
- preserve period-like links, spacing, raster decoration, and section structure;
- responsive behavior may prevent breakage on narrow screens, but must not redesign the page into a modern mobile layout.

## Quality check

Before finalizing, ask:

- Does this look like one person or small community made it?
- Does decoration reflect the author/topic?
- Are images small enough to feel plausible for the period?
- Are section names personal rather than productized?
- Is there evidence of manual updating or site history?
- Are guestbook/ICQ/counters/banners used as functional period elements rather than parody props?
- Did any modern card, hero, mobile-app, or SaaS pattern appear?

If the result looks like a modern page with GIFs pasted onto it, regenerate.

## Historical basis

This rule set was derived from a focused review of Russian personal and fan-made pages from roughly 2000–2004, including examples such as:

- `arudneva.narod.ru` — personal homepage with biography, family, friends and photo albums;
- `mouzes.narod.ru` — creative personal page with guestbook, author graphics, HotLog, Rambler Top100 and Рейтинг@Mail.ru;
- `smartpage.narod.ru` — text-heavy professional/personal site with publications and guestbook;
- `pers.narod.ru` — simple technical personal site;
- `aiesec.narod.ru` — small community site with guestbook, forum and site map;
- `geological.narod.ru` — hobby/community page with strongly thematic section naming;
- `spartak1550.narod.ru` — student-community page with poll, ICQ, birthdays and news;
- `biografie.narod.ru` — hobby/reference page with active guestbook;
- `warstar2000.narod.ru` / Star Warrior — thematic Star Wars fan page with dark palette, bright accents, 800×600/browser notes;
- `beyonce-knowles.narod.ru` — celebrity fan site with affiliates, banners, awards and repeated redesigns;
- `sneja-2002.narod.ru` — personal site with multiple 88×31 buttons and copyable link HTML.

Supporting research also includes the European University at St Petersburg project on the Narod.ru digital corpus, which documents the platform’s strong visual variability and DIY culture.
