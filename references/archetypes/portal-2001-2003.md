# portal-2001-2003

Historical design rules for Russian web portals, approximately 2001–2003.

Use this archetype for search portals, directories and large service hubs inspired by early Yandex, Rambler and Aport. The goal is a plausible Runet portal of the period, not a modern search homepage with retro decoration.

## Core idea

A Russian portal of 2001–2003 is a **start page for the Internet**.

It combines search with navigation to the wider web and to the portal's own services. Search is important, but the page is also expected to expose a large amount of useful information immediately: catalog categories, news, mail, weather, exchange rates, TV listings, shopping, regional links, rankings and other services.

Do not reduce this archetype to a large centered search field.

## Period split

### 2001

Default character:

- compact desktop layout designed around an 800×600-era screen;
- usually fixed or visually fixed width;
- commonly 2–3 information zones or columns;
- search near the top;
- catalog or ranking occupies a major part of the page;
- mail/login and news are visible without navigation;
- many blue underlined text links;
- colored title strips, thin borders and table-like grouping;
- little decorative whitespace;
- one prominent advertising banner is plausible;
- graphics are secondary to text and links.

The page should feel like a **directory + service dashboard + news board**, not a marketing landing page.

### 2003

Keep the density, but improve hierarchy.

Typical changes:

- search becomes more universal and visually important;
- portal services are presented as a coherent ecosystem;
- login/personalization becomes more visible;
- catalogs remain important but no longer have to dominate the whole page;
- news and service modules gain clearer hierarchy;
- styling can be more polished or slightly glossy;
- interfaces across different portal services become more consistent;
- fixed-width and fluid/rubber layouts coexist.

Do **not** enforce one width model for 2003. Rambler still used a fixed-width design, while other major portals were already moving toward fluid layouts.

## Page structure

A generated page should normally contain:

1. brand/logo area;
2. primary search form;
3. portal navigation or service links;
4. catalog, directory or Top-style ranking;
5. news block;
6. mail/login block;
7. 3–6 secondary service modules;
8. optional advertising block;
9. compact footer with corporate/service links.

Good secondary modules include:

- weather;
- currency rates;
- TV schedule;
- shopping / market / prices;
- images;
- regional links;
- gifts / postcards;
- software;
- entertainment;
- reference information;
- city services;
- partner links.

Do not include every module at once. Choose a coherent subset.

## Layout

### 2001 default

Prefer:

- fixed desktop container;
- 2–3 columns;
- narrow gutters;
- dense vertical stacking;
- visible section borders or title bars;
- content starting close to the top of the page.

The central or widest area should usually contain the catalog/ranking or the most important information block.

### 2003 default

Allow either:

- a refined fixed-width layout; or
- a fluid/rubber multi-column layout.

The choice should depend on the portal family, not on a universal rule.

Regardless of width, preserve high information density.

## Search

Search is always easy to find.

For 2001:

- a standard text input and ordinary button are preferred;
- search may offer simple scope switches such as web/news/catalog;
- it shares the first screen with many other portal functions.

For 2003:

- search may become the strongest visual element;
- one search interface can expose several search services;
- advanced search/help links remain textual and compact.

Never turn the page into a modern Google-like minimalist screen unless the brief specifically asks for a pure search engine rather than a portal.

## Catalogs and rankings

Catalogs are a defining feature of the early portal mode.

Use:

- two or more columns of category links;
- bold or linked category names;
- short subcategory lists after the main category;
- categories such as business, science, computers, culture, entertainment, society, sport, travel, work and shopping;
- rankings such as Top100 when appropriate.

For 2003, the catalog may occupy less space, but it should remain available in directory-heavy variants.

## Navigation

Navigation is primarily textual.

Prefer:

- horizontal service links;
- small text tabs;
- category lists;
- underlined hyperlinks;
- simple separators such as pipes, bullets and rules.

Small icons are acceptable, but they should support text rather than replace it.

Do not use icon-only navigation.

## Typography

Use ordinary system/browser typography.

Safe choices:

- Arial;
- Verdana;
- Tahoma;
- Times New Roman where appropriate.

Characteristics:

- small body text;
- compact line-height;
- headings only moderately larger than body text;
- bold text and colored bars create hierarchy more often than large type;
- blue underlined links are strongly appropriate, especially in 2001.

Do not use oversized display typography.

## Color and surfaces

The common base is light and utilitarian.

Prefer:

- white or very light page background;
- one strong brand color;
- one secondary accent color where needed;
- pale gray or colored section strips;
- 1px borders;
- minimal shadows.

Examples of historically plausible brand treatment:

- Yandex: white + yellow accents;
- Rambler: white + blue bars;
- Aport: white + blue/cyan accents.

Do not copy those palettes mechanically. The rule is **light information surface + strong brand accent**.

## Information density

High density is mandatory for the archetype.

The first screen should expose many separate destinations and pieces of information.

Achieve density through:

- short text links;
- compact lists;
- several adjacent service modules;
- small spacing;
- thin separators;
- multiple headings visible at once.

Density does not mean random clutter. Related items should remain grouped.

## Advertising

Advertising is historically normal.

Acceptable:

- one horizontal banner near the top;
- a smaller commercial block in a side column;
- text sponsorship or partner links.

Advertising should compete for attention more than on a modern utility site, but it should not cover the page or behave like a modern modal/interstitial.

## Personalization

For 2003 variants, allow:

- visible login/account block;
- unread-mail indicator;
- settings link;
- personalized news or portal preferences.

Keep these controls compact and textual.

## Graphics

Graphics are functional and small.

Use:

- logo;
- small weather icons;
- small news thumbnails when justified;
- tiny service icons;
- advertising banners.

Avoid:

- full-width photography;
- large hero illustrations;
- large decorative SVG icon systems;
- modern stock imagery.

## Internal variation

Keep one archetype. Do not create separate skills for each portal.

Use two lightweight internal tendencies:

### directory-heavy

Closer to Yandex 2001, Rambler Top100 and Aport:

- large catalog/ranking area;
- many category links;
- search + directory dominate;
- service modules surround them.

### service-heavy

Closer to the direction portals take by 2003:

- stronger portal navigation;
- more first-party services;
- personalization/login;
- news and utilities gain prominence;
- catalog remains but can shrink.

These are generation tendencies, not new archetypes.

## Required historical markers

A plausible result should contain several of these, not all:

- catalog of sites;
- Top100/rating;
- mail login;
- weather;
- currency rates;
- TV schedule;
- regional links;
- news headlines;
- search scope switches;
- small advertising banner;
- dense blue link lists;
- compact service menu;
- registration/settings link.

## Modern patterns to reject

Do not generate:

- giant hero section;
- huge centered search page with mostly empty space;
- rounded SaaS cards for every module;
- glassmorphism or blur;
- large border radii;
- modern dashboard tiles;
- mobile-first bottom navigation;
- icon-only menus;
- large modern illustrations;
- oversized typography;
- generous modern whitespace;
- floating action buttons;
- cookie banners or modern consent UI unless explicitly requested;
- modern marketing copy such as “Start free”, “Discover more”, or “Everything you need”.

## Generation procedure

1. Decide whether the target is closer to 2001 or 2003.
2. Choose `directory-heavy` or `service-heavy` tendency.
3. Put search near the top.
4. Add catalog/ranking unless the chosen 2003 service-heavy variant has a strong reason to reduce it.
5. Add news and mail/login.
6. Add 3–6 secondary service modules.
7. Use 2–3 dense information zones.
8. Apply light background, brand accent, thin borders and small text.
9. Optionally add one advertising banner.
10. Remove any modern card/hero/dashboard patterns that slipped in.

Primary and archival references:

Supporting historical context:

- Mail.ru portal consolidation in 2001 connected Mail.ru and List.ru through unified navigation;
- Russian portal redesigns of the period increasingly integrated first-party services and personalization.

## Research stop rule

This archetype is sufficiently defined for v1 when the rules above produce a dense Russian service portal recognizably different from both `narod-2002` and a modern search homepage.

Do not collect more historical examples unless implementation reveals a concrete unresolved question.
